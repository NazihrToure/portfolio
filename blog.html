01Learning

Building to learn, learning to build

Coursework gives me the foundations, but making something on my own is where the details start to click. A small page can teach a lot about layout, accessibility, and the decisions behind a finished product.

Read note ↗

When I start a project, I try to make the first version simple enough to finish. Then I look at what feels awkward: a layout that breaks on a phone, a button with no clear purpose, or a section that says too much at once.

Each pass gives me a more specific question to answer. That makes practice feel less like memorizing tools and more like learning how to solve problems.

02Design

What responsive design is teaching me

A design can look finished on a laptop and still have work to do on a smaller screen. Thinking about spacing, reading order, and tap targets early makes the whole experience clearer.

Read note ↗

I used to think of mobile styling as shrinking everything down. Now I look at which information matters first and how each section should flow when there is less space.

That shift helps on desktop too. If a page is easy to scan on a narrow screen, its structure is usually stronger everywhere.

03Process

Small improvements add up

Not every useful change is a new feature. Clearer copy, better contrast, or a more predictable navigation menu can make a project feel much more complete.

Read note ↗

My favorite part of revisiting a project is noticing things I missed the first time. Those details show what I have learned since I built it.

I keep a short list of improvements as I work. It helps me finish a version, come back with fresh eyes, and give the next change a clear purpose.
